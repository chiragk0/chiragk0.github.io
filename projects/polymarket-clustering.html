Clustering Market Behavior in Prediction Markets

ISYE 6740 Computational Data Analytics, Fall 2025 · Group project with Aditya Bansal

Spectral clusteringK-meansPCAANOVAChi-squareAdjusted Rand Index

My role

Data collection and preprocessing, PCA, spectral clustering and K-means implementation, statistical testing (ANOVA, chi-square, Adjusted Rand Index), visualizations, and the Methodology, Results and Algorithm Comparison sections of the report.

Problem

Prediction markets such as Polymarket should reflect the probability of real events, but individual markets behave very differently: some are stable and liquid, others swing wildly with little trading. The project asks what distinct behavior patterns exist, how many natural groups there are, and whether topics such as politics and crypto differ systematically.

Data and features

The analysis covers 300 completed markets across six topics (politics, cryptocurrency, sports, entertainment, science, business). Data caveat: API access problems during the project meant the markets are synthetic demonstration data built to match realistic ranges of volatility and volume, so the findings validate the method and not real Polymarket behavior.

Each market is described by eight standardized features covering volatility (price standard deviation, maximum single-day jump, average daily change), liquidity (mean and standard deviation of daily volume) and dynamics (momentum, consensus, duration).

Approach

PCA reduced the eight features to three components explaining 85.5% of variance, interpretable as volatility (39.8%), liquidity (26.1%) and consensus/duration (19.6%). Spectral clustering with an RBF kernel was the primary method and K-means the baseline. The number of clusters was chosen with silhouette, Davies-Bouldin and Calinski-Harabasz scores, and the two methods were compared with the Adjusted Rand Index.

Spectral clustering found one dominant stable cluster (272 of 300 markets) and three small, volatile or short-duration groups dominated by crypto markets. The two methods agreed weakly (Adjusted Rand Index 0.09, well under the 0.5 target), which points to non-spherical structure that favors the graph-based method. ANOVA showed all eight features differ across clusters (p < 0.001, largest F for volatility measures), and a chi-square test showed topic and cluster are not independent (χ² = 38.8, df = 15, p < 0.001).

Limitations: synthetic data, two clusters with only 5 and 2 markets, and a cross-sectional view that ignores how a market evolves over time.
