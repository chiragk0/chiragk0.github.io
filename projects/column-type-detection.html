Semantic Column Type Detection: Features vs. Transformers vs. Contrastive Learning

CS 7643 Deep Learning, Summer 2026 · Team of four (Robert Cole, Parth Chopra, Hadi Hajieghrary, Chirag Kulkarni)

PyTorchFeature engineeringGloVeDoc2VecFocal lossWeights & Biases

My role

Track 1 owner: Sherlock-style feature network, feature caching, loss ablation, experiment tracking.

Problem

Given only the cell values of a table column, predict one of 78 semantic types such as city, isbn or elevation. Labels come from the Sherlock/VizNet release, where columns were labeled by DBpedia-vocabulary matching. The project compares three model families under one shared training and evaluation pipeline, so differences reflect the model and not the harness.

Data

686,765 columns split into 412,059 train, 137,353 validation and 137,353 test. Per-class training support ranges from 584 to 9,088 (15.6:1), so the label distribution is flatter than a typical long tail. Sixteen low-support types were tracked separately as the tail-class metric.

My approach (Track 1)

Each column is converted to 1,588 engineered features: 27 global statistics, 960 character-distribution features (96 characters × 10 aggregates), 201 GloVe word-embedding features, and a 400-dimensional Doc2Vec paragraph vector. Separate subnetworks encode the character, word and paragraph groups (hidden sizes 300, 200, 400), and the statistics vector enters a 500-500 primary network directly. The model has roughly one million parameters.

I compared three configurations: the concatenation baseline with cross-entropy, a gated-fusion variant that learns per-example softmax weights over the three subnetworks, and the baseline with class-balanced focal loss. Feature extraction dominated cost, so I cached all features to memory-mapped arrays and trained from the cache.

Engineering fixes along the way: guarding non-finite statistics that produced NaN loss, replacing a failing histogram-based mode with a rounded-unique mode, and bounding the Doc2Vec vocabulary with a minimum word count (about 8.5 GB down to about 2.3 GB).

Results

Validation results, seed 13:

Baseline, concat, cross-entropy0.86850.84010.7404
Gated fusion, cross-entropy0.86170.82870.7133

The plain baseline was best on every metric. Its weighted F1 of 0.8683 is within two points of Sherlock's published 0.89. Gating and focal loss did not help, which is consistent with the flatter-than-expected label distribution. Per-feature-group ablations were not completed.
